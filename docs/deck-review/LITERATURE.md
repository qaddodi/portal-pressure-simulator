### Baveno VIII status and changes

Status: Baveno VIII is published. I could not open the full text because the fetch tool failed DNS lookup for every site in this sandbox. The details below come from search extracts of the Journal of Hepatology full-text page, the EASL and MedUni Wien press releases, GI and Hepatology News (AGA, September 2026), the Lancet Gastroenterology and Hepatology editorial 'Baveno VIII: what's new?' (2026) and Echosens (a vendor). LoE grades and agreement percentages quoted below come from those journal-page extracts.

Citation: Baveno VIII Faculty, 'Baveno VIII - Advancing consensus in portal hypertension', J Hepatol 2026, doi 10.1016/j.jhep.2026.07.030, PMID 42624290, PII S0168-8278(26)02778-9.
- Authorship is collective. Named authors include Berzigotti, Mandorfer, Pons, Abraldes, Ripoll, Reiberger and de Franchis.
- Dates: accepted 30 July 2026, online about 19-25 August 2026 (EASL release 25 August), print in vol 85 issue 5 (November 2026).
- Process: Baveno, March 2026; 84 experts in 9 panels; 272 statements with more than 80% agreement; 153 research priorities. Each statement is tagged new, modified or unchanged against Baveno VII (de Franchis et al., J Hepatol 2022;76:959-974).

Changes versus Baveno VII:
1) Terminology: cACLD is now the preferred term whenever the diagnosis rests on non-invasive tests. The rule of five (10-15-20-25 kPa) is reaffirmed. The rule-out (LSM 15 kPa or less with platelets 150 or more, NPV over 90%) is unchanged.
2) CSPH rule-in can be any one of:
   - LSM 25 kPa or more, only in viral, alcohol-related or non-obese (BMI under 30) MASLD;
   - a predicted probability of 75% or more from ANTICIPATE, ANTICIPATE-NASH (MASLD with BMI 30 or more) or the new NICER model;
   - spleen stiffness above 55 kPa on the 100 Hz VCTE probe (Baveno VII: under 21 kPa ruled CSPH out, over 50 kPa ruled it in).
   Indeterminate results are reassessed after 12 months.
3) NSBB, 'particularly carvedilol', in cACLD with CSPH 'to prevent decompensation and improve survival' (LoE 2, strong, modified, 97%). Not for patients without CSPH. No screening endoscopy while on an NSBB.
4) New: with ascites and varices, carvedilol or a conventional NSBB, carvedilol preferred, to prevent a first bleed and lower ascites-related complications (LoE 3, strong, new, 81%). Reduce the dose or stop if systolic pressure is under 90 mmHg, mean arterial pressure is under 65 mmHg and/or HRS-AKI develops.
5) Statins: downgraded. There is now insufficient evidence to recommend them for preventing decompensation.
6) Acute variceal bleeding:
   - Vasoactive drug for 2-5 days or until TIPS (LoE 1, modified). A course as short as 24 h may be considered after successful hemostasis if an NSBB is started (LoE 2).
   - Antibiotics at presentation, third-generation cephalosporin preferred. Duration individualized; Child-Pugh A may need a short course or none (LoE 2, weak, modified).
   - Restrictive transfusion to hemoglobin 7-8 g/dL, individualized (LoE 2, strong).
   - Endoscopy within 12 h.
   - New: erythromycin 250 mg IV 30-90 min before endoscopy (LoE 2, weak, 94%).
   - EVL for esophageal varices and GOV1.
   - Pre-emptive TIPS (PTFE-covered) within 72 h, ideally under 24 h, for Child-Pugh C 10-13, Child-Pugh B over 7 with active bleeding, or HVPG 20 mmHg or more (Baveno VII said over 20) (LoE 1, strong, modified, 93%).
   - New 'late' window: TIPS within 2 weeks (C 10-13) or 1 week (B over 7 with active bleeding) may still help (LoE 4, weak, 88%).
7) Recompensation:
   - Criteria must last more than 6 consecutive months (LoE 4); Baveno VII required 12.
   - Encephalopathy is judged off lactulose, rifaximin or LOLA.
   - Liver function must return to Child-Pugh A. This is from GI and Hepatology News; the journal extract did not show it.
   - Follow-up every 6 months with HCC surveillance continues after recompensation.
8) New MASLD section:
   - Weight loss of 10% or more goes with a fall in LSM of 30% or more (LoE 3).
   - GLP-1 receptor agonists and SGLT2 inhibitors for approved indications.
   - Bariatric surgery only in selected patients without CSPH.
9) First pediatric statements: LSM or SSM alone should not guide management in children.
10) Vascular panels (PVT, Budd-Chiari) were updated. I did not verify their content.

Not found in what I could reach: Baveno VIII wording on post-SVR discharge, gastric varices and BRTO, TIPS stent diameter, rescue-TIPS futility, and secondary prophylaxis (it appears carried forward).

| HVPG definition and thresholds | The HVPG is the wedged minus the free hepatic vein pressure, a measure of the sinusoidal portal pressure gradient. Normal is 1 to 5 mmHg, 6 to 9 mmHg is portal hypertension that is still subclinical, and 10 mmHg or more is clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH), from which varices, ascites and other decompensating events become possible. The risk of a variceal bleed rises from about 12 mmHg (a rising risk, not a cut-off), 16 mmHg or more carries a higher risk of death, and 20 mmHg or more during an acute bleed predicts failure to control it and is a Baveno VIII criterion for pre-emptive TIPS. ### Still evolving

- BOPPP (UK, 740 patients, carvedilol 6.25 mg against placebo for small varices; the primary endpoint changed to first decompensation). I found no published results as of October 2026. Protocol: Trials 2024, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11022376/
- REACT-AVB (UK, 294 patients, Child-Pugh 7 to 13; early TIPS against standard care; primary endpoint 1-year transplant-free survival) has not reported. It will test whether Baveno VIII's Child-Pugh B criterion holds. Protocol: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10966777/
- Pre-emptive TIPS in Child-Pugh B with active bleeding, and the new late window: the 2024 individual-patient meta-analysis favors TIPS (HR 0.43), but single trials are mixed (Dunne 2020, no survival benefit). The 1 to 2 week late window rests on LoE 4, weak evidence. Hepatology 2024;79:624-635; Baveno VIII 2026.
- Fundal varices: GAVAPROSEC (Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2025) found less rebleeding but no survival gain with pre-emptive TIPS against glue. Baveno VIII's pre-emptive TIPS statement names only esophageal varices and GOV1. EUS-guided coil plus glue, and retrograde obliteration against TIPS, still lack head-to-head trials. https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03705078
- Carvedilol against banding for high-risk varices remains unsettled: CALIBRE (Aliment Pharmacol Ther 2025) was stopped at 10% of its target and is underpowered. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12074564/
- Beta-blockers in decompensated and refractory ascites: Baveno VIII's new LoE 3 recommendation rests on small trials such as CARVE-AS (Am J Gastroenterol 2026, 104 patients, open-label). The safe 'window' and the blood pressure and kidney stop rules remain debated.
- CSPH gray zone: Baveno VIII uses a 75% model probability (ANTICIPATE, ANTICIPATE-NASH or NICER) and spleen stiffness above 55 kPa at 100 Hz. AASLD 2024 treats LSM 20 to 25 kPa with platelets under 150, or 15 to 20 kPa with platelets under 110, as presumed CSPH. The 100 Hz probe is not widely available, and there are no cut-offs for non-VCTE elastography.
- MASLD: the HVPG may underestimate portal pressure (Ferrusquia-Acosta, J Hepatol 2021), yet it predicts outcome (Paternostro, J Hepatol 2024). MAESTRO-NASH OUTCOMES (resmetirom, 845 patients with compensated MASH cirrhosis) is expected around 2027 (https://www.hcplive.com/view/phase-3-maestro-nafld-1-data-resmetirom-mash-cirrhosis-with-naim-alkhouri-md). The role of GLP-1 receptor agonists in cirrhosis is unsettled.
- Statins: Baveno VIII downgraded them after LIVERHOPE (JAMA 2025) and StatLiver were negative. SACRED (VA, simvastatin 40 mg in compensated CSPH, NCT03654053) is active and not recruiting, with results pending. https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03654053
- TIPS diameter: 8 mm against underdilated 10 mm against controlled-expansion grafts. Encephalopathy falls with smaller stents (Wang, J Hepatol 2017; JHEP Reports 2025), but ascites control may suffer (Riggio, J Hepatol 2010). EASL 2025 says to use the smallest diameter that reaches a gradient under 12 mmHg. A registered trial compares underdilated VCX-TIPS with banding plus a beta-blocker for secondary prophylaxis (NCT07587671).
- Shorter vasoactive and antibiotic courses: Baveno VIII allows a 24-hour vasoactive course after successful banding once a beta-blocker starts, and a short antibiotic course or none in Child-Pugh A. AASLD and ESGE still give 2 to 5 days and up to 7 days. Trials are ongoing (for example NCT05199038, 2 against 5 days of octreotide).
- Recompensation: Baveno VIII shortened the required duration to 6 months (LoE 4). Expanded criteria (Tonon, J Hepatol 2025) capture 37.6% against 7%. Open questions remain whether the beta-blocker can stop after recompensation and which laboratory cut-offs define restored function.
- Long-term albumin (ANSWER positive, PRECIOSA neutral on its primary endpoint, EASL 2025) and anticoagulation (CIRROXABAN, J Hepatol 2025, with a signal in Child-Pugh B7) as disease-modifying adjuncts remain unproven.
- Baveno VIII full text: several items (Child-Pugh A for recompensation, spleen stiffness under 40 kPa to spare endoscopy, gastric variceal and TIPS-diameter statements) are confirmed only through secondary summaries. A line-by-line check of the J Hepatol article (doi 10.1016/j.jhep.2026.07.030) is still needed.

Changes versus Baveno VII that bear on this assignment:
(1) Recompensation. All of these must hold for more than 6 consecutive months (Baveno VII asked for 12): the cause removed, suppressed or cured; no ascites on imaging off diuretics; no overt HE off lactulose, rifaximin or LOLA; no recurrent variceal bleeding. Liver function must also return to Child-Pugh A. Ascites resolving while on diuretics does not count (LoE 2, 93% agreement; and LoE 5). recompensation.js:62-64 already matches this.
(2) Decompensation. Ascites seen only on imaging, covert HE and occult bleeding from portal hypertensive gastropathy are not decompensation.
(3) NSBB. Carvedilol is the preferred NSBB, and the indication is CSPH itself, not varices. One excerpt reports a new statement that carvedilol is preferred over other NSBB in patients with ascites and varices (LoE 3, strong, 81%); this is low confidence. Statins were downgraded: evidence is insufficient to recommend them for preventing decompensation.
(4) TIPS. HCC is not an absolute contraindication to TIPS for recurrent or refractory ascites or hydrothorax unless the tumor lies on the track. TIPS is not recommended to prevent decompensation in compensated cirrhosis with CSPH.
(5) Pre-emptive TIPS. A covered TIPS within 72 h, ideally within 24 h, for Child-Pugh C 10-13, Child-Pugh B above 7 with active bleeding, or HVPG of 20 mmHg or more (LoE 1, strong, 93%). TIPS within 1 to 2 weeks may still help some patients who miss that window. Red-cell transfusion target unchanged at hemoglobin 7-8 g/dL, individualized.
(6) PVT with acute variceal bleeding. Manage it as without PVT. TIPS with or without portal vein recanalization may be considered when there is ascites, documented PVT progression or transplant candidacy (LoE 5, strong, modified, 85%).
(7) Anticoagulation (vascular panel, which covers BCS, PSVD/NCPF and PVT). When indicated, start it promptly and do not delay it for endoscopic variceal prophylaxis (strong). Stop it during active variceal bleeding and restart on an individual basis. Start with LMWH and then switch to an oral agent (weak). A DOAC is the first-line oral agent except with double or triple positive antiphospholipid syndrome, pregnancy, Child-Pugh C or GFR under 30 mL/min (weak). A multidisciplinary team reviews the treatment regularly (strong).
(8) HE after TIPS. For HE that recurs or persists despite standard therapy, consider embolizing a spontaneous shunt and/or reducing or occluding the TIPS (LoE 3, weak, new, 87%).
(9) Screening endoscopy in cACLD patients not on an NSBB. Endoscopy can be skipped when LSM is under 20 kPa with platelets of 150 x 10^9/L or more, or when spleen stiffness is under 40 kPa (LoE 1, strong, modified, 100%). Patients already on an NSBB need no screening.
(10) First pediatric section. Liver and spleen stiffness alone should not yet guide management of portal hypertension in children.

### Still evolving

- Long-term albumin. ANSWER (Lancet 2018) found a survival benefit. ATTIRE (NEJM 2021), MACHT (J Hepatol 2018) and PRECIOSA (EASL 2025 abstract; 1-year HR 0.80, p = 0.17) did not, although PRECIOSA showed early and secondary signals. The full PRECIOSA paper, a post hoc look at who benefits (pre-treatment albumin, PMC13355858) and the 2026 UEG J commentary 'Not an answer yet' (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ueg2.70278) are what to watch. Until then, tap.js:86 should not imply it is standard care.
- HRS-AKI diagnostic window. ICA-ADQI 2024 shortened the volume challenge to 24 h and dropped the fixed 48 h albumin of 1 g/kg (J Hepatol 2024;81:163-183). Critics report that 18 to 28% of eventual albumin responders do not respond early, so a 24 h window may overdiagnose HRS and overtreat with terlipressin (J Hepatol 2024, S0168-8278(24)02422-X; J Hepatol 2025, S0168-8278(25)00164-3, on albumin duration).
- Terlipressin delivery and safety. The US label allows IV boluses only (Terlivaz label, rev. June 2026). Europe often uses a continuous infusion, which may cause fewer adverse events (Cavallin, Hepatology 2016). The respiratory risk in ACLF grade 3 and volume overload (CONFIRM, NEJM 2021) still limits its use in the US.
- Beta-blockers in ascites. Excerpts report that Baveno VIII may now prefer carvedilol even with ascites and varices (LoE 3, 81%); this is unverified. A 2026 US claims cohort of 26,128 patients found about 20% fewer decompensating events with carvedilol than with nadolol at 6 months (observational). The 'window hypothesis' for refractory ascites is still unsettled (Baveno VIII J Hepatol 2026; GI and Hepatology News Sept 2026).
- TIPS for ascites: when and how wide. Trials compare earlier TIPS for recurrent ascites (Bureau 2017), 8 against 10 mm stents (Radiology 2017: 10 mm controlled ascites better without more HE), under-dilation and selection scores (FIPS, MOTS) (EASL TIPS CPG 2025). Residual ascites 3 months after TIPS predicts worse outcomes (PMC12167471).
- Embolizing spontaneous shunts. There are no randomized trials; 2026 meta-analyses show about 82% HE improvement but 10 to 15% new varices, ascites or bleeding (PMID 42564112). Trials are testing embolization at the time of TIPS and coil-assisted shunt reduction for post-TIPS HE (NCT07799948). ACG 2026 gives a conditional recommendation with very low certainty.
- Primary prevention of HE. Bausch's two RED-C phase 3 trials of rifaximin SSD did not delay a first HE episode (announced 23 Jan 2026; https://ir.bauschhealth.com/news-releases/2026/01-23-2026-124516159). Rifaximin stays a drug for preventing recurrence, and for prophylaxis around TIPS (Bureau, Ann Intern Med 2021).
- Anticoagulation beyond PVT. CIRROXABAN (rivaroxaban 10 mg in Child-Pugh B cirrhosis; J Hepatol 2025, S0168827825023360) missed significance on its primary endpoint but showed benefit in Child-Pugh B7 (HR 0.26) and more minor bleeding. EASL 2025 states that anticoagulation may lower morbidity and mortality in Child-Pugh B and C cirrhosis without PVT (LoE 2). DOAC safety in Child-Pugh B is still being studied.
- PSVD. Noninvasive diagnosis (multiparametric ultrasound, NCT07492862), the minimum biopsy length (15 against 20 mm), TIPS for complicated PSVD (NCT07163689) and the role of anticoagulation without PVT are all open questions. Revised VALDIG criteria may follow EASL 2025 and Baveno VIII.
- SOS prophylaxis. After HARMONY (Lancet Haematol 2023, negative), EASL 2025 advises against defibrotide prophylaxis and recommends ursodeoxycholic acid for all HSCT. The EBMT 2023 probable, clinical and proven criteria are still being validated (PMC10994840).
- Fontan-associated liver disease. There is no consensus on staging, on which stiffness or MR elastography cut-offs to use, or on HCC surveillance intervals (EASL-ERN 2023; Frontiers in Medicine 2025 editorial). Liver biopsy during routine catheterization is being proposed.
- Pregnancy with cirrhosis. ALBI (UKOSS 2025, AUROC 0.80) may predict decompensation better than MELD. Data on carvedilol in pregnancy are thin, and propranolol remains the drug of choice (AASLD 2021; EASL 2023).
- TIPS Doppler for covered stents. Thresholds come from the bare-stent era and have poor specificity. A composite Doppler score for early TIPS dysfunction was published in 2026 (PMID 42525340). Mitchell 2025 (Abdom Radiol) shows that velocities depend on stent diameter.
- Devices for refractory ascites. The FDA approved the alfapump in Dec 2024 on a single-arm study (POSEIDON, n = 69), and its place relative to TIPS and transplant in US practice is undefined.
- Portopulmonary hypertension and the US MELD exception. OPTN Policy 9.5.G was revised in 2020 to admit some candidates with treated mean pressure of 35 mmHg or more. Check the current criteria before teaching the 35 mmHg and 400 dyn.s.cm-5 numbers (https://optn.transplant.hrsa.gov/media/4251/further_nlrb_enhancements_202012.pdf).
- Cirrhotic cardiomyopathy. The 2019 criteria are widely used, but their prognostic value is uncertain: in a 2025 cohort, 29% met the criteria, mostly by strain, with no link to outcomes.
